# Workflow and decision gates

This file applies when producing a new edit or changing an existing one. An approved plan settles the intended story and style, but approximate timecodes and factual statements still need footage checks.

## 1. Input and evidence pass

- Resolve source paths without modifying the files. Probe width, height, coded and display aspect, nominal and average fps, rotation/display matrix, audio streams, duration, and decoding errors. A landscape encoded frame may display vertically; do not choose crop based on dimensions alone. Preserve source fps when appropriate, and conform mixed/VFR clips deliberately on an integer-frame timeline.
- Extract plan sequence, title copy, roles, source in/out, backups, evidence constraints, required tracks and formats. Make a manifest with source path, source range, timeline range, frames, role, media type, transform, asset lineage, and evidence note.
- Use contact sheets for discovery. Confirm pivotal actions in dense samples or actual playback with enough lead-in/out to show causality. A single still cannot prove a selection, retrieval, or reaction. The `inspect-raw-video` skill can inventory footage but its approximate intervals are not final editorial evidence.
- Classify each planned claim: visible; human-confirmed but not self-evident; uncertain; contradicted. Use titles accordingly. Never add a fake motion, object, text or conclusive verdict to solve an evidence gap. If the real object exists on camera, show that shot before any explanatory title.
- If a decisive shot is longer or elsewhere than planned, keep the action continuous when that is necessary for proof. Reallocate timing and disclose the exact deviation. Choose an alternate only when it fills the same role and evidence standard; list it in the report.

## 2. Offline picture cut and review

- Build in frames, not rounded seconds. Treat source out convention explicitly. Keep shot IDs stable from plan through manifest/report.
- Reframe representative beginning/middle/end frames of every important shot at output resolution. Check faces, eyes, hands, and every object or piece of text the story depends on. A shifted crop can be a cut between adjacent source segments with no source frames omitted. Do not use blurred or clipped shots as long explanatory inserts.
- For short-form pacing, use immediate hook, readable quick cuts, small push/zoom or gentle speed ramp where it strengthens attention, and restrained whooshes. Do not infer a required effect merely from the style label.
- Keep original sync audio and, if pacing needs it, a temporary bed or click. Put simple title placeholders at their intended timing and reserve visual space for them. This exposes rhythm and crop conflicts before expensive sound/graphics work. Final mixing and title animation can wait for a stable cut.
- A derived proxy for a punch-in, speed ramp, or real-frame freeze is acceptable if original source remains in the project or is relinkable and the derivation is reported. Describe it as a rendered insert, not a native keyframe effect. Prefer native Resolve elements when practical for future manual changes.
- When source choices, cuts, crop, and total frame count are stable internally, export a versioned review reference. Call it a candidate cut, not picture lock. Have an independent producer review the rendered candidate against the goal with the `roles/producer.md` lens, fix factual/technical blockers, and give the user the preview plus remaining recommendations. Obtain the appropriate editorial/client approval before declaring a full picture lock. For a short creator-owned piece, user approval of the rendered cut is that gate unless explicitly delegated. Some shots or sections may be individually approved earlier.
- Final sound and graphics follow picture approval. A prototype on a candidate cut or an approved section is fine when the user wants speed over rework, with the revision risk stated in the handoff. A later timing change invalidates affected work; issue a changed-frame list instead of silently reusing old assets.

## 3. Specialist passes and integration

Sound and graphics may run in separate agents or sequentially once they have an approved picture lock or approved section (or, for a prototype, a clearly labelled provisional reference). They need the same small handoff, not the whole footage-analysis history: version/approval status, fps, resolution, start timecode, total frames, shot IDs and timeline ranges, event markers in frames, exact title copy, evidence restrictions, safe-area notes, output file paths, and a reference render with visible timecode when practical. Add original audio clips, editable timeline/AAF with handles when crossing applications, and a guide mix to the sound handoff; add representative stills/crop boxes and editable design assets/font details to the graphics handoff. The owner of the Resolve timeline alone integrates the returned assets unless actual shared-project locking and responsibilities have been set up; agents should not concurrently save the same standalone project.

- Sound designer: retain or deliberately attenuate original audio; work from separate source audio clips where available, with a guide mix for comparison. Create separate music, SFX, and voice tracks/stems with known zero at timeline frame 0, sample rate, version ID, and a cue sheet keyed to frame numbers. Duck under useful speech and build accents at picture events. Deliver source/rights information and peak/loudness readings. Audition against picture before calling it final. A single flattened A1 stem is only a fallback or guide, not an equivalent replacement for editable production sound.
- Graphics/VFX artist: generate missing **presentation** assets from approved copy/colors, with transparent backgrounds and explicit start/end frames. Preserve source PNG/SVG or editable generator parameters. Verify mobile readability and that overlays avoid faces and the objects or text the story depends on; never create a substitute action or evidence. Return an asset map and any crop conflict to the timeline owner.
- Timeline owner/online finisher: import specialist outputs to separate tracks, relink/conform to intended source quality, align starts and event cues, verify alpha, crops, grade continuity, and audio sync, then re-render. If the cut changes, send only the delta plus updated reference to affected specialists. Keep graphics separate from picture and retain editable text/source assets when future versions are expected. Check the finished master, not just each specialist's isolated deliverable.

- Keep separate role tracks if requested: original sync audio, music, SFX, and voice. The exact track set is plan-specific. Never silently discard original audio; mute or attenuate with a report if unusable. A flattened source-audio stem is less editable than per-clip sync sound, so disclose it.
- Duck music where speech might matter. Place SFX at events observed on final timeline, then audition the rendered composite. Check true peak/sample peak and clipping; avoid treating one target loudness as a universal rule.
- Assemble with explicit timeline frame positions and source references. Read [resolve-api.md](resolve-api.md) for tested scripting behavior. Save project and export fresh output paths; verify each API result instead of assuming it succeeded.

## 4. Rendered QA and handoff

- Decode preview and master in full. Check dimensions, actual video frame count/fps, presence and codec of audio, black frames at individual cuts, and level peaks with [qa_render.py](../scripts/qa_render.py). AAC container duration may exceed picture duration slightly due to padding; compare video frames.
- Watch the whole vertical render and separately the hook/first three seconds, proof shot, numbered objects, final image, every tight crop, and transition boundaries. Check safe area against phone UI and ensure titles do not cover evidence. Automated checks cannot establish these.
- Check sound accents by listening at their picture events. Inspect the master as well as the preview; do not assume they are visually identical merely because one renders.
- Report actual duration; every used original and source in/out; alternates; evidence-critical shot range; effects/transitions; audio sources and treatment; quality issues; deviations and source replacements; editability limits; XML/Final Cut manual checks. Mark incomplete deliverables honestly.

## Model routing with an approved design

Route by judgment needed, not by prestige: the session that owns the timeline executes the approved picture design and evidence review; an independent producer reviews the **rendered** candidate for goal fit and audience comprehension; a cheap mechanical subagent (`vk-errand` on Claude Code) can run the deterministic Resolve preview command and return its verified file path. The renderer must not make editorial decisions or declare picture lock. Give each role one phase and the smallest sufficient handoff. Escalate ambiguous visual evidence, missing action, crop conflicts, unclear audio/words, or final editorial judgment to the user. For the role split behind this, see [industry-patterns.md](industry-patterns.md). Keep observations and decisions in the manifest/report so another model can resume without reconstructing the conversation. Independent sound and graphics branches can prototype on versioned candidates or approved sections; final work follows the relevant lock when revision cost is high. Use branches only when their work outweighs setup/integration cost. One owner reconciles IDs, timings, evidence and final output. Do not let branches independently rewrite the approved story.
