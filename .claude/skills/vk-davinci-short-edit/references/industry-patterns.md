# What professional post workflows actually support

Evidence checked 2026-10-12. These sources describe film, broadcast, and commercial workflows; the proposed AI role split is an adaptation for small social edits, not a claim that every facility uses these exact agents.

## Source-backed patterns

1. **Offline editorial and picture approval.** The [Frame.io post workflow guide](https://workflow.frame.io/guide/editing-stages) describes picture lock as the point at which editing decisions stop changing so sound, color, and finishing can proceed. Its [conform pitfalls guide](https://workflow.frame.io/guide/offline-workflow-pitfalls) details the downstream cost of a late cut change. An internally stable timeline is not automatically client-approved picture lock.
2. **Parallelism is normal, with version control.** Blackmagic's [Resolve collaboration documentation](https://www.blackmagicdesign.com/uk/products/davinciresolve/fairlight) says sound, edit, color, and VFX staff can work at the same time using shared-project locking and timeline comparison. A [documented commercial workflow](https://workflow.frame.io/guide/broadcast-ad-workflow-sunoco) shows sound design evolving with editorial and selected shots/sequences going to VFX before full lock. Therefore do not require every specialist to wait for the entire film to lock; mark provisional versus approved material.
3. **Sound handoff preserves editability.** [Avid's AAF export guidance](https://kb.avid.com/pkb/articles/en_US/Knowledge/How-to-export-an-AAF?popup=true&retURL=%2Fpkb%2Farticles%2Fen_US%2FKnowledge%2Fen336549%3FretURL%3D%252Fpkb%252Farticles%252Fen_US%252Fcomp_1) distinguishes media linking, full-copy, consolidated media with handles, and flattened mixdowns. Avid's [audio/video interoperability guide](https://resources.avid.com/supportfiles/attach/AudioVideo_Editing_Workflows.pdf) discusses a guide audio track to keep picture and sound in sync. For a one-application Resolve job, preserve per-clip source audio; for a cross-application turnover, send an editable exchange with suitable handles plus a reference movie/guide mix. A flattened A1 stem can be a guide or fallback but narrows revision options.
4. **Online/finishing is a distinct verification pass.** The [Frame.io conform guide](https://workflow.frame.io/guide/common-conform-issues) calls for project files and separate graphics layers; it warns that baked titles complicate grading and handoff. The [Frame.io workflow overview](https://workflow.frame.io/guide) describes conforming the offline recipe to source-quality media, with sound, color, VFX, and final delivery checks. For a 30-second Resolve edit, a separate facility and formal conform may be unnecessary, but source relinking, alpha, grade, sound, and export QC still have to be checked.
5. **Graphics templates support versions.** Adobe's [broadcast graphics workflow](https://helpx.adobe.com/premiere/desktop/collaborate-with-others/collaborate-using-productions/incorporate-branded-graphics-and-audio-elements.html) describes graphics shared with editorial for consistent versioned output. An alpha render is convenient, but editable text/source artwork and font details should accompany it when wording or language variants may change.

## Adaptation for this skill

Use four responsibilities rather than forcing four simultaneous agents: (a) assistant editor/media and evidence inventory, (b) picture editor/story/crop and review cut, (c) sound and graphics specialists on versioned references, and (d) online finisher/QC. One capable agent can hold several roles on a small job. A separate agent pays off when its context is narrow and output is a stable asset or report; spawning one to make a single tick or title adds overhead. The picture editor owns story truth and shot timing. Specialist branches never alter that narrative or independently save the same standalone project. The finisher verifies the assembled result and records deviations.
